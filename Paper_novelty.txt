NOVELTY STATEMENT - PLANNED ADDITIONAL WORK BEYOND THE BASE PAPER
=================================================================
Base paper: "Explaining building damage from wildfires in California",
            Bar, Li, Banerjee. Science Advances 12, eaed4197 (2026).
Student:    see student_info.txt

1. WHAT THE BASE PAPER ALREADY DOES 
----------------------------------------------------------
Random Forest on 100,228 CAL FIRE DINS records, three feature sets (comprehensive,
enviro-weather, environmental exposure), SHAP and causal-forest analysis, a
200 m spatial dead-zone cross-validation (88 % accuracy for the comprehensive
model), three held-out fires (76-82 %), leave-one-grid-out 1-degree blocks
(68 %), and a 100 m risk map.

2. GAPS FOUND IN THE BASE PAPER AND ITS DATA
--------------------------------------------
G1. The headline 88 % comes from a 200 m exclusion zone only. Buildings a few
    kilometres away, in the same fire, can still sit in both train and test.
    The paper's other tests (3 fires, 1-degree blocks) give much lower scores,
    but no single analysis shows how accuracy falls as test data gets farther
    from the training data.
G2. In the supplied dataset, 78.9 % of rows (79,062 of 100,228) have the fire
    name "No Fire Name Found", so fire-level validation is impossible for most
    records.
G3. Damage01 = 1 also contains 436 buildings labelled "Inaccessible" (not
    inspected). About 91 % of the remaining damage class is "Destroyed (>50%)";
    affected, minor and major together are only about 5,000 rows.
G4. Aspect and wind direction (0-360 degrees) and the vegetation-type code (EVT)
    are used as ordinary numbers, although they are circular / categorical.
G5. Weather variables (tdmean, Tmax, VPD, Srad) are strongly correlated
    (r >= 0.70 in the paper), so per-variable importance splits credit between
    them.
G6. The risk map is read as a probability, but calibration and prediction
    uncertainty under spatial shift are not evaluated.

3. PLANNED CONTRIBUTIONS (novelty of this work)
-----------------------------------------------
C1. Multi-scale spatial generalisation audit  (answers G1, G2)
    Re-evaluate the same models with increasing separation: random split,
    200 m dead zone, 1 km / 5 km / 25 km buffers, spatial blocks of 0.25 and
    1 degree, and leave-one-named-fire-out. Fire events missing from the data
    are reconstructed by clustering building coordinates. Output: an accuracy-
    versus-distance curve and a recommended reporting standard.
    Pilot result (60-tree forest, 5 folds, no SMOTE): random split 0.928
    accuracy / 0.980 AUC, but 0.25-degree spatial blocks only 0.808 accuracy /
    0.873 AUC (SD across folds 0.089).

C2. Label and data-quality audit  (answers G3)
    Remove or separately model the "Inaccessible" records, and report results
    both for "any damage" and for "destroyed versus no damage".
    Pilot result: dropping the 436 Inaccessible rows gave 0.823 accuracy /
    0.881 AUC versus 0.808 / 0.873 (indicative only: test sets differ slightly).

C3. Better feature representation  (answers G4)
    Encode aspect and wind direction as sine/cosine, flag flat terrain
    (aspect = -1), and treat EVT as a categorical variable.
    Pilot result: sine/cosine gave only +0.005 accuracy, within fold-to-fold
    variation, so this will be reported as an ablation, not a main claim.

C4. Model comparison and uncertainty under spatial shift  (answers G6)
    Compare the Random Forest with gradient-boosted trees, then measure
    calibration (Brier score, expected calibration error, reliability curves)
    and conformal prediction sets, checking whether the promised coverage holds
    on spatially unseen areas.
    Pilot result: the forest's probabilities are fairly well calibrated overall
    (expected calibration error 0.043 under spatial blocks), so the main
    question is coverage under spatial shift, not recalibration.

C5. Group-level explanation robust to correlation  (answers G5)
    Report grouped permutation importance and grouped SHAP for five groups
    (topography, ecosystem, human footprint, building, weather).
    Pilot result: removing the building-flammability rating (CBFR) lowers
    spatial-block accuracy from 0.808 to 0.750 and AUC from 0.873 to 0.791.

C6. Reproducible, tested, deployed software  (completed in this repository)
    Saved model bundle, validated Flask API, Angular form, automated tests, and
    CI/CD with GitHub Pages, so every reported number can be regenerated.

5. EVALUATION PLAN
------------------
Metrics: accuracy, precision, recall, F1, ROC-AUC, Brier score, calibration
error. Report mean and standard deviation across spatial folds, and compare
variants with paired tests across the same folds. Same 21 comprehensive-model
features for all comparisons, with Model 01 (no CBFR) and Model 02 (static
only) as references.

6. LIMITS AND HONEST CLAIMS
---------------------------
- Pilot numbers above are indicative only: smaller forest, 5 folds, no SMOTE,
  no tuning. Final numbers will use the full protocol.
- The dataset has no date column, so true temporal validation (train on early
  years, test on later years) is left as future work.
- The CBFR rating was calibrated by the paper's authors using damage outcomes
  (30 % sample). Its exact calibration subset is not in the data, so possible
  leakage cannot be fully ruled out and will be stated as a limitation.
- This work does not re-implement the paper's causal forest or its CBFR
  derivation.
